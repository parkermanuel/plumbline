# Church Planter Assessment: How the Major North American Organizations Do It

Research compiled 22 Oct 2026 to inform a free, original planter + spouse assessment with a graded report for network leaders. Everything below is paraphrased. No proprietary items are reproduced. Where a public page didn't disclose something, it's marked "not public".

---

## 1. Stadia ("Discovery" / Church Planter Assessment)

**Process (3 phases + event)**
1. **Connect:** interest form, then a phone call with Stadia staff.
2. **Exploration:** self-paced work on "Am I called?" covering personal calling, input from the candidate's community, and gifts.
3. **Discernment:** "Am I ready?" The candidate describes the church they hope to start, reviews holistic health, names strengths and growth areas, submits a written Discernment Reflection, and interviews with Stadia staff. An event invitation follows within ~5 days of that interview.
4. **Discovery Event:** 3 days, in person, mostly paperless (bring a device). Individual and group activities let candidates explore, demonstrate and strengthen competencies.
5. **Report + next steps:** a "robust report" with growth recommendations and a recommendation on next steps. Continuing planters get a project manager, Essential Training, coaching, cohorts and post-launch care.

**Observer requirement:** the candidate brings an observer who knows their current ministry (pastor, elder, network or denominational leader). The observer gives feedback during the event and in older formats served as an assessor. This builds the sending relationship into the assessment itself.

**Competency model (4 domains, 20 competencies, refined over 20+ years, described as model-agnostic):**
- **Gospel:** vibrant faith, fluency, fervor, communication, empowerment ethics
- **Cultural:** awareness, humility, contextualization, proficiency, engagement
- **Relational:** interpersonal communication, conflict resolution, relationship building, social-emotional health, empathy
- **Entrepreneurial:** visioning, motivation, resilience, empowering, influence

**Spouse:** must take part in the *entire* process from Exploration on. Some items are submitted separately and some jointly, and the spouse attends the event. Stadia also runs "Bloom," a care and development ministry for planter spouses, which planters single out for praise.

**Outcomes:** historically approve / defer with specific growth areas / decline. Criteria included giftedness, marriage solidity and theological grounding, with the stated purpose of "finding areas for growth."
**Cost:** usually covered by the sending church, network or denomination. Stadia works with candidates who lack a sponsor. Candidate pays travel and lodging. C&MA districts have advertised it as free to the candidate.
**Who uses it:** Stadia planters plus partner denominations (e.g., C&MA Metro District sends candidates into the Stadia assessment).
**Claims/praise:** self-reported 90% "success rate" (undefined metric). Spouse care (Bloom) is consistently praised.
**What it does best:** a staged discernment funnel (called, then ready, then demonstrated), spouse involvement from day one, and an observer from the home church.

## 2. Send Network / NAMB (Southern Baptist)

**History:** The Launch Network (Mac Lake, Chief Launch Officer) built the outline and content in 2011 and assessed just under 200 planters from 2011 to 2014. NAMB took it over in 2015 and made it "more rigorous." Tim Wheat standardized formats that had varied widely before 2011. Older NAMB-era instruments included the Church Planter Profile (personal, doctrinal and lifestyle data from both spouses) and inventories derived from the Church Leader Inventory research (see PCA below). PREPARE-ENRICH is still required for married and engaged couples.

**Current pathway (2024–2026 public pages and state convention pages):**
1. **Interest form / application ("Send Me"):** due 90 days before the retreat. Includes references, background check, **credit check**, and MinistrySafe abuse-prevention training.
2. **Pre-assessment:** three online questionnaires seen by the candidate, the sending church and NAMB, covering **church planting capacity, personal character and marriage health**.
3. **Send Portal instruments (~25 days to complete, due 60 days out):** a Church Planter Assessment, **DISC**, and a **Church Planter 360** (multi-rater).
4. **Candidate questionnaires (~2 days):** calling and vision, leadership, missional engagement, communication, emotional and spiritual health.
5. **Pre-retreat modules (~2 weeks):** a recorded mini-sermon plus a 3-minute vision cast, a 7-day calling module, and a 7-day self-leadership module.
6. **Hard stop at 40 days out:** everything complete.
7. **Two-day Assessment Retreat** in or near the target region, with about 6 couples and about 8 trained assessors. Day 1 is back-to-back interviews (planters call it "intense"). Candidates preach and present their vision, then field questions. There are large-group exercises. Couples get a date-night gift card on night one.
8. **Sending church representative must attend.** They observe and get breakouts on how to sponsor, coach and care for the planter.

**Spouse (separate instruments):** calling and health questionnaires, a Spouse Church Planter Assessment, Spouse DISC, Spouse Church Planter 360, and PREPARE-ENRICH. She attends the retreat, and the couple is assessed together on family dynamics. Leaders state plainly that "if the spouse isn't ready, the planter isn't ready."

**Nine competencies:** calling, emotional and spiritual health, family dynamics, vision, leadership, communication, missional engagement, disciple-making, and social skills / relational intelligence.

**Outcomes:** currently a three-tier color system. **Ready (green)**, **Development Needed (yellow)** with a written growth plan, timeframe and reassessment, and **Redirect (red)**. Older and some state versions use four tiers: Ready, Ready with Conditions (coach on a named concern), Further Development Needed (internship + plan), and Cautioned. In 2016 about half of prospects got green or yellow. Today few get "redirect" because pre-screening narrows the field first.
**Report delivery:** a **Final Assessment Report** goes to the sponsor or sending church. Assessors talk through next steps and a growth plan with the couple face to face before they leave.
**After:** Ready planters join a training cohort and get a coach and care team. Launch is typically 6–18 months later. Send City planters get a 3-day orientation paid by NAMB.
**Cost:** no candidate fee is published. NAMB reimburses some travel. A virtual retreat and Spanish-language retreats exist.
**Timing:** at least several months end to end, with 29+ retreat locations and quarterly dates.
**Praise:** thorough, done "in love," affirms calling, couples report important conversations about calling and expectations.
**Criticism:** heavy front-loaded paperwork and deadlines, intensity of the interview day, and the credit check and background screening feel invasive to some.
**What it does best:** a multi-source evidence package (self + 360 + spouse + references + observed performance), the sending church in the room, and clear color outcomes tied to a development plan and reassessment date.

## 3. ARC (Association of Related Churches)

**Process:**
1. **ARC Launch event:** required first step with no prerequisites. Training led by ARC pastors and coaches, ideally 12–18 months before launch.
2. **Online Application:** after the event. Couples take about 2–3 weeks, then a rotating Review Team (coaches, pastors, staff) takes about 3 weeks.
3. **Before the in-person event:** a "deeper marriage assessment," online training modules, and a drafted launch plan.
4. **In-person Assessment Event:** **both spouses must attend.** Assessors work with the couple on marriage strengths and growth areas.
5. **Train** (All Access online platform), **Coach** (regional launch coach who planted an ARC church; monthly webinars), **Launch**.

**Three domains: Marriage, Money (personal financial stewardship), Ministry.** The motto is "healthy couples plant healthy churches."
**Launch thresholds for funding:** a launch team of 45 adults, raise $50k to get a $50k match (zero-interest, "pay it forward" through an automatic monthly draw), a sponsoring church that guarantees repayment, and 3 outside Overseers acting as the early board. Plants then give 2% monthly to ARC.
**Timing:** about 60–90 days from Launch event to Assessment.
**Cost:** fees not public.
**Criticisms (Roys Report podcast, former ARC planter):** high-profile pastors were allegedly fast-tracked and their finances not examined. Out-of-town overseers who meet about twice a year can't provide real accountability. The model emphasizes attendance and launch-day size, and his launch of 68 people shrank to 12.
**What it does best:** treats **money** (personal finances) as a first-class domain, makes marriage central, and attaches concrete measurable launch thresholds to readiness.

## 4. Orchard Group (NYC, Restoration Movement)

- Founded 1948, describes itself as "highly selective" about planters and cities.
- Historically used the **Church Planting Assessment Center (CPAC)** model shared by many Christian Church / Restoration Movement groups. That model uses core competencies, interviews, psychological testing and practical exercises, and produces a development plan.
- After selection, a **coach plus a "management team"** stays with the plant until it is self-supporting and self-governing. Orchard openly says assessments are "not perfect," so coaching and a management team are the safety net.
- Public details on scoring and spouse process are sparse.
**What it does best:** pairs assessment with a long-tail accountability structure. It treats the assessment result as a starting hypothesis that ongoing oversight confirms.

## 5. Strategic Launch Network (SLN, launchstrong.com, Rockwall TX)

- Started from Lakepointe Church (Steve Stroope). The first plant was NYC in 2002, and it became its own 501(c)(3) in 2007. President is Jason Williams. The board includes pastors from Lakepointe, Saddleback, Traders Point and others.
- Focus is strategic, hard-to-reach North American cities (NYC, DC, SF, Boston, Denver, Phoenix, Portland, Montreal).
- Self-reported: 90+ churches, 5,841 baptisms in 2025, 64k+ weekly attendance, and a "96%" success rate (elsewhere "95%+"; inconsistent and undefined).
- **Pathway:** Assess, Select, Strategize, Connect, Launch, Sustain.
  - **Assess:** starts as a *listening conversation* about story, theology, vision and calling, followed by evaluation of character, family and readiness. Their stated value is that "fit matters more than a fast start."
  - **Select:** framed as a *mutual* commitment, not a formality. They select a limited number of planters per year.
  - **Strategize:** city launch plan covering demographics, funding model, team, timeline.
  - **Connect:** coach (a seasoned planter, monthly calls), partner churches, cohort.
  - **Sustain:** coaching and financial support for about 3 years, then annual retreats and peer community.
- Lakepointe's approach historically used **personality profiles** and successful planters as vetters, and prioritized pastor-leader qualities and conservative theology.
- **Instruments:** SLN does not publicly name its instruments. I could not verify Predictive Index, CliftonStrengths or Enneagram use from public sources, so ask SLN directly. Its "robustness" appears to come from **high selectivity + multi-stage relational vetting + 3-year funded coaching**, not from a published test battery.
**What it does best:** a relational, mutual-fit selection philosophy, small cohorts, and a long, funded support runway.

## 6. Other organizations

### Acts 29
- **Pipeline:** Recruit, Train (residency or apprenticeship), **Assess** (3–6 months), Coach. More than 1,600 planters assessed.
- **Steps:** online application with a submitted sermon, online questionnaires, peer references, then an **Assessment Conference** (3 days).
  - **Preaching assessment:** an assessor reviews the sermon in advance, writes a preaching report and holds a video call.
  - **Pastoral case study:** discussed in a group.
  - **Strategic plan presentation:** candidates present their plan and assessors question it.
  - **Two interviews** with the candidate's own assessment team (older format: about 2 hours with 3 planters).
  - **Wives:** informal discussions with *female assessors* about ministry expectations. Historically couples were also interviewed together.
- **Criteria:** calling, character (elder qualifications in 1 Tim 3 / Titus 1), theology and competency, leadership capacity, missional experience.
- **Outcomes (historic):** accepted / accepted with conditions / not now (work about a year, then reassess) / not acceptable. A shorter, informal reassessment is used later.
- **How "no" is delivered (Matt Adair):** decide as a team only after the full process. Deliver quickly, clearly and personally, by phone or video, not just in writing. Separate the *assignment* from the person's identity and calling, offer coaching toward a fitting assignment, and expect grief.
- **Testimonials:** "rigorous, difficult… rewarding." It clarified calling *and timing* and strengthened marriages. A wife's account (Yepishin) described a "not now" that felt like "maybe never," then a re-assessment two years later to a different city. In hindsight she called it grace, especially for self-knowledge growth.

### Assemblies of God / Church Multiplication Network (CMN)
- AG districts used the **Ridley Behavioral Assessment interview** plus personality tests and standard interviews.
- CMN today focuses on training (Launch), 9-month cohorts with monthly coaching (urban and rural tracks), and **matching funds** (more than $22M given). It has trained 8,500+ people and been part of 5,000+ launches. Public detail on CMN's assessment step is limited.
- The AG *Enrichment Journal* (2009) summarizes the Stetzer data on assessment, bootcamp, mentoring and peer groups (see predictors below).

### Converge
- **Church Planting Assessment Center:** 4 days, staffed by trained assessors, with a counselor on site. Steps: district contact, pre-assessment interview, application, district registers the candidate.
- The couple attends together. Exercises include preaching, vision casting and team settings, plus counselor sessions.
- Roles are explicit: candidates, sending-agency assessors (each agency usually sends one), non-assessing observers, and a director, counselor and administrator.

### PCA Mission to North America (Assessment Center / "Ministry Discovery")
- 2.5–3 day intensive with experiential exercises, simulations, interviews and instruments. **Multiple trained observers pool data into one global evaluation per person. No quota, no competition.**
- **Planter model:** 10 dimensions / 34 competencies from the **Church Leader Inventory research** (doctoral study of 68 successful planters): integrity, personal spiritual dynamics, missional engagement, visioning capacity, gospel communication, learning agility, emotional stability, family life, expectant of results, managerial courage. It adds **"stallers/stoppers"** (derailers): self-centeredness, poor relating to context or people, not building talent, trouble with results.
- **Separate spouse model:** 6 dimensions / 13 competencies (spiritual dynamics, family life, integrity, learning agility, missional engagement, emotional stability), with its own derailers (no sense of call to church ministry, self-centeredness, performance-based spirituality, poor relational skills).
- Newer Ministry Discovery framing: Gospel (spiritual vitality, zeal for the lost, preaching), Emotional (EQ, cultural intelligence, resilience), Relational (family dynamics, relational dynamics, integrity), Leadership (team dynamics, entrepreneurial, vision casting).
- **Planter-wife research (Shari Thomas, PCA, 2005):** 34 wives of *successful* plants responded. About 65% named their husband as primary emotional support. Formal denominational support was the lowest-rated area (2.4/5) and denominational training scored 2.1/5. 60% led a major ministry, and almost none were paid. Several said the **assessment center was the only training they ever got**. It made them feel like valued partners, but they wanted follow-up in the early years and many got none. Recommendations: a spouse support structure, 3-year mentoring with a yearly retreat, marriage training for both spouses, boundaries and exhaustion training.

### Christian & Missionary Alliance (C&MA)
- District-run. Several districts send candidates through **Stadia's assessment** after district approval (Discover, Determine via Stadia assessment, Develop, Deploy). Stadia assessments have been offered free to C&MA candidates.

### Vineyard USA
- Assessment is step 2 of 4: multi-step, combining self-assessment with external evaluation. Results drive *customized* training in step 3. Instruments and spouse role are not public.

### Redeemer City to City (CTC)
- Focus is training (Church Planting Program, incubators, intensives) across 20+ denominations. Assessment is largely delegated to the planter's denomination or network. Public detail is minimal.

### NewThing (Community Christian Church)
- **Residency, not assessment, is the main filter:** about 9+ months, a 40-week curriculum, about 3 hours a week of class, and field mentoring. Phases are unlearn, learn (team leadership, reproduction, teaching, vision casting, fundraising), send.
- Calling must be established *before* residency. Self-funding is "the first hurdle," a real-world readiness test. Their lesson: some residents were sent too early.

### Exponential
- Offers church-level and leader-level tools such as the **Hero Maker Assessment**: 30–60 minutes, gives an immediate Level 1–5 score, and is described as both training and assessment. It also has a Multiplication (5-level) and Mobilization assessment. These are not planter-selection tools but are a good model for **instant, leveled, developmental scoring**.

### Wesleyan Church (CPAC)
- Indianapolis assessment center (in-person or Zoom) plus a "wiring" assessment built from 5–6 personality inventories. Results route people to *roles* (large launch, dinner church, house church, team member, funder) rather than pass/fail. Volume rose from 29 to 59 a year after adding virtual.

### Church of the Nazarene (USA/Canada) – Ridley-based Assessment Center
- Optional online pre-assessment co-developed with LifeWay ($29). Then a 4-day center: personality analysis, private interviews, sermons, case studies, strategy development, worship. Couples attend together. **$500 per couple** ($750 for non-Nazarenes), including room and board.
- **Four outcomes:** unconditional approval / conditional approval (context or training) / defer at least 2 years / not recommended.

### Fellowship of Evangelical Churches (FEC)
- 2-day center for planter + spouse. Group and individual exercises and interviews. Assesses competencies **and relative health in spiritual life, marriage and finances**. **$500.**

### EFCA ReachNetwork
- Interest form, extensive pre-assessment (all candidates), then a regional assessment retreat by invitation, then a **results meeting where the candidate and spouse each receive their own comprehensive report.** EFCA also uses the LifeWay CPCA for early discernment.

### Bible Fellowship Church (sample workbook, public PDF)
- Couple-based: pre-discussion of the call, doctrinal review, planter profile. Then a discussion of competencies, plant types and "cost to the family." Then a **~3-hour behavioral interview** (written answers prepared in advance) across about 16 areas. Evaluators write narrative assets and liabilities plus up to 5 recommendations, and the director reviews them with the couple. **No numeric scales.** This is an example of a lightweight, narrative approach.

### Church at Brook Hills (J.D. Payne writeups) – sending-church model
- "Big Three" first: an **informed-consent agreement** (sets expectations because the process is personal and emotional), a Church Planter Profile from **both spouses** with a follow-up interview that *starts with affirmation*, and a background check.
- Then a small-group leader's assessment of both spouses (based on *The Barnabas Factors*), PREPARE-ENRICH, and a custom leadership assessment (Foxworthy & Associates / The Lion's Lead) taken by both spouses across 9 areas. That assessment is described as "lengthy, thorough, and pricey."

## 7. Foundational frameworks

### Charles Ridley – 13 behavioral dimensions (late 1980s, Indiana University)
- Built from studying effective planters across 13 Protestant denominations in the US and Canada. It became the basis for "nearly every" network's assessment center for decades.
- **The 13:** visioning capacity, intrinsic motivation, creating ownership of ministry, relating to the unchurched, spousal cooperation, effectively building relationships, commitment to church growth, responsiveness to community, utilizing the giftedness of others, flexibility and adaptability, building group cohesiveness, resilience, exercising faith.
- **Method: behavioral interviewing,** based on the principle that past behavior best predicts future behavior. Ask for specific real situations ("tell me about a time…"), not hypotheticals ("what would you do…"). Ridley trains interviewers in multi-day seminars to write role-specific questions.
- **Common self-check version:** rate each of the 13 from 1 (rarely shown) to 4 (passionately shown) by **self, a friend, a supervisor and a ministry recipient**, then total them. Published bands run roughly: >180 ready, 161–180 ready after work on weak areas, 141–160 proceed with caution, ≤140 probably not ready. **Look for discrepancies between raters.**
- Several networks adapted it into 15-trait rubrics. One network (C2C) named 5 **non-negotiables**: emotional health, relational ability, marriage and family strength, integrity, and relationship with God.

### Thomas Graham – Church Leader Inventory lineage
- Graham's work (assessment-center methodology used by PCA/MNA and others since the 1990s) underlies the **Church Leader Inventory** competency research. That research produced the PCA's 10-dimension / 34-competency planter model and a separate spouse model, and it is credited as the source for Send Network's early CLI/CLSI-style instruments. Public pages describe the model but not Graham's original documents. Treat attribution details as needing confirmation.
- Key structural ideas: **competencies plus derailers ("stallers/stoppers")**, multiple trained observers, pooled data, one global rating, no quotas, and an **explicit spouse competency model** (not just "spousal cooperation" as a line item for the planter).

### LifeWay Church Planter Candidate Assessment (CPCA, ~2011, Ed Stetzer)
- Sponsored by 11 denominations and networks (NAMB, AG, EFCA, PCA, etc.). Built from interviews with 30 assessment experts, expert item review, and **3 rounds of quantitative testing with 500+ planters and pastors.**
- 20+ statistically reliable scales. Online, scored instantly, results sent to the sponsoring organization. About $88 (or $29 in a discounted partner version).
- Stetzer positioned it as an **initial indicator, not a pass/fail verdict**, and noted that defining "success" is itself hard.
- **Criticism:** an experienced two-time planter and entrepreneur "failed" an online planting assessment (Rohadi Nagassar) and questioned its cost and its fit for non-traditional planters.

### Asbury "Church Planter EQ" self-assessment
- 8 factors in two groups, adapted from Bradberry and Greaves's EQ model. Personal competence: personal health (emotional, physical, financial), spiritual, vocational (calling), visional. Social competence: relational, cultural, missional, adaptable. Framed as a snapshot, not pass/fail.

## 8. Research-backed predictors of plant survival and growth

| Factor | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Being assessed** | Assessed planters' churches averaged about **20% larger** in each of years 1–4 (**27%** larger in year 3). By year 4 they averaged about **25 conversions a year vs about 12** for non-assessed planters. | Stetzer dissertation (601 SBC plants, 2003), via Baptist Press and AG *Enrichment Journal* |
| **Realistic expectations** | Survival **87% vs 61%** when the planter's expectations matched reality. Reported as "400%+" higher odds. | NAMB/Leadership Network Church Plant Survivability study (2007; about 500 surveys, 12 denominations) |
| **Leadership development for members** | Survival **79% vs 59%** (">250%" odds). | Same 2007 study |
| **Monthly planter peer group** | Survival **83% vs 67%** ("135%" odds). In the 2003 data, peer-group planters' year-1 churches were **more than twice as large**. | 2007 study; Stetzer 2003 |
| **Proactive stewardship plan toward self-sufficiency** | Survival **81% vs 68%** ("178%" odds). | 2007 study |
| **Mentor / coach / supervisor** | Churches **12% larger in year 1, rising to 25% larger in year 4**. **Weekly** meetings produced churches about 50% larger than monthly or quarterly. About 60% of planters had one; 74% had a denominational mentor in 2007. | Stetzer 2003; 2007 study |
| **Basic training / bootcamp** | 30% larger in year 3 and 27% larger in year 4 (no first-year edge). | Stetzer 2003 |
| **Planter's wife working outside the church** | Associated with **significantly slower growth and lower attendance** (correlational; may reflect financial need). | Stetzer 2003 |
| **Adequate planter pay + health insurance** | Linked to higher attendance and a greater chance of financial independence. | LifeWay 2015 National Church Planting Study (843 plants, 17 sponsors) |
| **Multiplying early** | Plants that start another church within 3–5 years have higher attendance and more conversions. | 2007 and 2015 studies |
| **Membership expectations, new-member training, covenant** | Larger in every year. | Stetzer 2003; 2007 "10 factors" |
| **Multiple staff** | Two staff roughly doubled attendance vs one by year 4. | Stetzer 2003 |
| **Funding amount** | Did **not** clearly predict eventual size. | Stetzer 2003 |
| **Overall survival** | **68%** of plants still existed after 4 years (about 4,000 plants a year), refuting the "80% fail in year 1" myth. 54% were self-sufficient by year 3. | State of Church Planting USA (LifeWay / Leadership Network, 2007) |
| **Teams** | 88% of successful plants had planting teams. 63% of fast-growing plants raised extra funding vs 23% of struggling plants. | Stephen Gray, *Planting Fast-Growing Churches* |
| **Infrastructure** | 68% of denominational agencies and 75% of networks have formal assessment processes. | LifeWay / Leadership Network |

**Spouse wellbeing context (LifeWay 2017, 720 pastors' spouses, ±3.7%):** 69% have few people to confide in. 50% avoid confiding at church because of past betrayal. 62% rely mainly on their spouse under stress, and only 9% on other ministry spouses. 36% worry monthly about making ends meet and 60% say pay doesn't support the family. 86% feel expected to have a "model marriage." 72% say their spouse has faced resistance in the church. *(General pastors' spouses, not planters specifically, but directly relevant to spouse-assessment design.)*

**Caveats:** most quantitative data is from 2003–2015 and is SBC-heavy. Findings are correlational, the "odds increase" percentages don't map cleanly onto the raw survival rates (cite the raw rates), and network "success rates" (Stadia 90%, SLN 95–96%) are self-reported and undefined.

## 9. Cross-organization synthesis

**Common process skeleton (nearly universal):**
1. Interest / application, including informed consent, background check, sometimes a credit check.
2. Online self-inventories: personality (DISC, etc.), planter competencies, calling and health questionnaires.
3. Multi-rater input: references, 360, supervisor, small-group leader, a person they've ministered to.
4. Marriage instrument (PREPARE-ENRICH is the most common).
5. Work samples: sermon, vision cast, launch plan.
6. Behavioral interviews (Ridley method), often 2+ with different assessors.
7. Observed exercises: case study, team task, presentation Q&A.
8. Assessor consensus into one recommendation, plus a growth plan, plus a reassessment date.
9. Debrief in person or by phone or video; the report goes to the sponsor or sending church.
10. Handoff to coach, cohort and care.

**Common outcome scales:**
- 3-tier: Ready / Development Needed / Redirect.
- 4-tier: Ready / Ready with Conditions / Further Development / Cautioned or Not Recommended.
- Some add an explicit "defer X years."

**Spouse approaches, from weakest to strongest:**
- (a) "spousal cooperation" is one line item on the planter's rating
- (b) spouse attends and is interviewed with the planter
- (c) spouse takes parallel instruments (Send: spouse assessment, DISC and 360; ARC: deeper marriage assessment)
- (d) **a separate spouse competency model with its own derailers** (PCA)
- (e) **each spouse gets their own report** (EFCA)
- Best practice combines (c) through (e), plus female assessors and spouse follow-up care (Stadia Bloom).

**What planters praise:** clarity on calling *and timing*, a growth plan, being treated as a couple, kindness in delivery, and relationships with assessors.
**What they criticize:** cost and travel, paperwork load, intensity, opaque criteria, "failing" an online test despite a track record, uneven rigor (celebrity fast-tracking), no follow-up for spouses after assessment, and online tools that don't fit non-traditional or bivocational models.
